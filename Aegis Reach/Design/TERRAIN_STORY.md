# AEGIS REACH // TERRAIN STORY GRAMMAR

## Goal

Vesper should communicate history before dialogue does. Terrain is not filler between combat arenas; it is one of the main narrative systems in the game.

The player should be able to look across a space and infer:

- where water used to move;
- which structures predate the military occupation;
- where people actually worked and sheltered;
- which routes humans built because the geology forced them to;
- where the moon is behaving in a way geology alone cannot explain.

## Relayfall exterior composition

The south side of Relayfall now resolves into a nested terrain sequence rather than one flat floor:

1. **Fortress threshold** — the military wall interrupts an older landscape.
2. **Inner evaporite basin** — broad pale fighting floor with long sightlines.
3. **Old brine channel** — darker sinuous material band with repeated salt rims.
4. **Meridian causeway** — civilian infrastructure bridging the channel.
5. **Basalt benches** — broad raised geological shelves that create flank routes and firing positions.
6. **Outer shelf** — slightly lower, quieter terrain opening the horizon beyond the first combat bowl.
7. **Distant rim** — non-colliding silhouettes that keep the moon larger than the playable boundary.

The player should never feel like they are walking across a generated heightfield. Vesper uses large geological sentences, not visual noise.

## Environmental timeline

### Layer 1: Vesper

Dark basalt and pale evaporite establish the moon's natural history. Long channels, terraces, and directional salt deposition imply recurring tidal cycles driven by Aurelia.

### Layer 2: Meridian Survey

The survey crews responded to the terrain instead of bulldozing it flat.

Their causeway crosses an old channel. Shelter 12 sits near geothermal activity. Tide gauges and survey paint preserve water-height history. The field cairn is tiny because real people leave small evidence behind, not giant lore monuments.

### Layer 3: AEGIS

The fortress is newer and more indifferent. Its wall cuts across the older civilian route. Military lighting and relay architecture overlay the survey landscape rather than replacing it.

### Layer 4: Iron Warden occupation

Temporary firing positions, blocked routes, consumed supplies, and tactical patrols should continue to read as occupation decisions rather than original construction.

### Layer 5: The Choir

The Choir must initially look like geology that is *slightly too regular*.

`RESONANCE CUT 03` exposes a set of nearly parallel black ribs inside a tidal fracture. It is intentionally not a glowing alien shrine. Close inspection produces only a faint mineral response and one restrained Kestrel reaction.

The desired player thought is:

> That cannot be natural.

Not:

> Here is the alien plot object.

## Repetition rules

### Historic waterline

The same approximate mineral elevation should recur on:

- old retaining walls;
- tide gauges;
- survey shelter surfaces;
- exterior fort structures that predate the final military rebuild;
- later underground access architecture.

Consistency lets the player infer the vanished sea without exposition.

### Prevailing wind

Salt and dust should collect on leeward faces in the same direction. Recesses, door pockets, cable trays, and the underside of old machinery should remain darker.

### Civilian cyan vs AEGIS cyan

Civilian Meridian cyan is faded, chalky, and scratched. Active AEGIS cyan is clean, emissive, and electrically saturated.

They should feel related but not identical.

## Combat use

Terrain must earn its collision.

The inner shelf should support:

- a straight dangerous crossing;
- a west-side basalt flank;
- an east-side elevated answer to the anchor enemy;
- a readable fallback to the fortress breach;
- long-range use of the marksman rifle found near Mira's wreck.

The outer shelf is deliberately less populated. It exists first as scale, quiet, and future mission space.

## Emotional rhythm

The exterior should play approximately as:

**reveal -> orientation -> curiosity -> optional detour -> contact -> tactical choice -> release -> deeper curiosity**

The survey shelter and resonance cut should not compete with active combat. Their Lua reactions deliberately wait until the local fight is quiet.

## Asset rule

Every future terrain prop must answer at least two of these questions:

1. What geological process put this here?
2. Why did a human need this object in this exact location?
3. How did the old brine sea alter it?
4. How did the military reuse or ignore it?
5. How does it change movement, sightline, or combat choice?
6. What does it imply without dialogue?

If the only answer is “it looks sci-fi,” do not place it.
