# FIRST LIGHT // Visual Target

The target is not "more props." It is a readable, authored sci-fi campaign space embedded in Vesper's terrain. **The title screen is a visual contract for the mission's larger Northstar silhouette**: cold blue night, fortified industrial massing, cyan relay landmarks, warm pockets of human light, and the broken orbital-array crown behind the installation. The playable level does not need to reproduce the menu image literally, but it must look like the same place and the same production.

## Survey Camp 12 north star

Camp 12 should read at a glance as a compact remote Meridian survey outpost built where the terrain provides shelter.

The composition should have:

- one dominant field-lab / habitat mass tucked against a terrain cut or ridge;
- a protected human-scale work court rather than an open prop yard;
- a small survey mast and a few deliberately grouped instruments;
- one compact power/logistics edge, not repeated containers everywhere;
- warm amber practical lighting around occupied/work areas;
- restrained cyan/blue technical accents around survey systems and lab infrastructure;
- an obvious route entering and leaving the camp without floating labels doing the navigation work;
- large-scale Vesper geology, sky and distant planetary/orbital forms carrying much of the visual drama.

The environment should feel closer to an Antarctic research station, expedition base or forward science outpost translated into a grounded science-fiction setting than to a campground, construction yard, urban street or sandbox prop pile.

## Northstar / title-screen convergence

Camp 12's restraint does **not** define the entire mission. Northstar is the visual payoff and should grow into the fortified installation promised by the title screen.

Northstar should carry, at minimum:

- a dominant broken orbital-array / crown silhouette visible well before the player reaches the facility;
- several tall cyan-accent relay spires establishing vertical rhythm and navigational hierarchy;
- layered bunker, retaining-wall and service-deck massing that makes the site read as infrastructure rather than scattered props;
- cold navy/blue environmental dominance with small amber practical pools where humans worked;
- enough distant and edge massing to imply a larger installation beyond the exact playable lanes;
- preserved combat negative space: skyline density belongs behind and beside traversal, not inside the authored firing lanes or landing zone.

The initial convergence layer may use visual-only, non-colliding original Aegis Reach assets for distant massing. Native MAX screenshots remain authoritative for their scale, occlusion and silhouette placement.

## Composition hierarchy

From the player's initial approach, visual priority should be:

1. the field-lab / shelter mass;
2. the terrain enclosure around it;
3. the survey/technical landmark;
4. the warm human work zone;
5. the road continuing toward Gate 07.

As the route opens toward Northstar, priority shifts to:

1. the broken crown-array silhouette;
2. the primary relay/spire hierarchy;
3. the fortified facility mass;
4. warm/cold lighting contrast;
5. the readable service route through that mass.

Everything else supports those reads.

## Density rule

A finished-looking scene does not require a high entity count. Prefer:

- large coherent forms;
- strong negative space;
- purposeful functional clusters;
- lighting pools;
- terrain integration;
- restrained repetition.

Delete before adding when the composition becomes noisy. **Do not confuse negative space with an empty skyline**: Northstar can be visually dense at distance while the combat floor remains clean.

## Material and faction language

**Meridian civilian:** functional, compact, survey/science driven, warmer practical lighting, restrained technical blue.

**Iron Warden occupation:** barriers, reinforced logistics, harsher military additions layered onto existing civilian spaces.

**AEGIS:** cleaner, colder, more monumental and more vertically controlled.

**Choir:** visually incompatible with the human kit; geology and impossible repeating structure rather than generic ancient-alien ornament.

## Quality gate

A production screenshot should make sense with HUD labels hidden.

Ask:

- Does the location have one unmistakable visual anchor?
- Does the terrain explain why the structure is there?
- Can I see the intended route without an objective arrow?
- Do the lights imply where people worked and lived?
- Are props grouped by believable function?
- Is there enough empty space for the eye and combat sandbox to breathe?
- Does Northstar plausibly belong to the same world shown on the title screen?
- Does the image feel like one place rather than several asset packs colliding?

This visual target is aspirational. Native GameGuru MAX screenshots remain the authority for scale, pivot, material and final composition decisions.
